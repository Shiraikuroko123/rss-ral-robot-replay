01 / THE QUESTION

One hidden state creates two different research problems.

A replay controller may hold a Cartesian target that the policy cannot directly observe. A sender can reconstruct what it believes it wrote, while the receiver may contain a different target after an overwritten or dropped input. The two papers examine this ambiguity at different levels.

RSSWHY / WHEN

When does target readback change the replay?

Readback reduces worst-case target error only when origin-specific corrections are reachable under the controller's action limits. RSS then follows the corrected write through first contact and final object outcome.

Open the RSS study ↓
RA-LWHAT / HOW MUCH

When is hand feedback enough?

RA-L separates target-writing error from physical tracking error. It tests whether hand measurements, command history, and sparse reads recover the same replay outcomes across Fetch, Sawyer, and Panda.

02 / THE PAPERS

RSS submission draft

When Does Reading a Cartesian Target Change Robot Replay?

Zhangwei Chen · Robotics: Science and Systems submission draft

A minimax analysis identifies when one readback can reduce the next bounded target-write error. Paired Sawyer pushing experiments then show that a changed target can rescue a rollout, break another, or leave the final outcome unchanged because contact geometry dominates.

When Does Controller Target Readback Help Cartesian Demonstration Replay?

Zhangwei Chen · IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters manuscript

A cross-embodiment replay study tests where the error lives. Hand-based reconstruction recovers failed Fetch and Panda replays without a read, while event-triggered reads on Sawyer reduce hand-path error by 0.039 mm without changing the 144 terminal outcomes.

04 / HOW THEY FIT

A shared setup, two non-overlapping conclusions.

01

RSS asks whether a read changes the physical replay. Its main evidence is contact propagation and paired task outcome.

02

RA-L asks where the error comes from and whether a read is necessary. Its main evidence is cross-embodiment reconstruction and observer diagnosis.

03

Both papers keep their claims bounded. Neither claims real-robot deployment or a universal readback policy.
